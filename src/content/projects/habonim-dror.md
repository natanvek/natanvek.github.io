## The organization

Habonim Dror is a youth movement that runs educational programs and camps for kids and teenagers.
In Argentina it operates across six sites: **Buenos Aires, Rosario, Tucumán, Resistencia, Corrientes, and Mar del Plata**.

## National Treasurer

My biggest role in the movement, and a full-time job in practice. I was responsible for the movement's
finances at the national level, which meant making sure every camp, trip, and program could actually happen.

### Leading a national team
I led a team of **local treasurers, one at each of the six sites**. I coordinated how each site handled its money,
kept everyone working from the same budget, and supported them when local problems came up.

### Fundraising
I **designed and ran funding campaigns** to finance the movement's activities and keep them accessible to the
families taking part.

### Negotiating with vendors
National camps of **300+ participants** depend on outside suppliers. I **negotiated directly with campsites,
transportation companies, and catering providers**, balancing cost, quality, and the safety of the kids we were responsible for.

### Budgets and logistics for national camps
I built and managed the budgets for national camps and ran the logistics that move hundreds of people,
from six different cities, to the same place and back.

### Leadership programs
Alongside the finances, I delivered **leadership programs for teenagers**, training the next generation of counselors.

## Before that: Community Director

I trained as a Community Director in 2018–19 and led educational activities for younger members,
which is where I learned to run a group before running a budget.

## What it taught me

Handling real money for real people at national scale, with a team spread across the country and hard deadlines,
is a different kind of optimization problem, and one I found just as rewarding as the technical ones.
It taught me to negotiate, to delegate, and to be accountable for decisions that affect hundreds of families.
